01

Overview

Studying a director by making.

This short was created for a class assignment asking us to study a director through a small original film. I chose Quentin Tarantino and focused on handheld camera movement and colour.

I was particularly interested in the final scene of Inglourious Basterds. My goal was not to reproduce the scene, but to test how visual choices could create a similar contrast between a nonchalant character and a stressed one.

the question

“Could the camera and colour communicate the emotional mismatch before the dialogue did?”

02

Process

How I approached it

  1. 01

    Study the reference

    I looked at handheld movement, framing, colour, and the different energy assigned to each character.

  2. 02

    Storyboard the contrast

    I planned the scene around the emotional dichotomy rather than treating the storyboard as a list of attractive shots.

  3. 03

    Film and edit

    I used movement, pacing, colour, and audio to shape the final interaction, then reviewed what carried through successfully.

03

Outcome

A complete short—and a clearer visual vocabulary.

The project resulted in a finished short film and gave me a practical way to understand directorial choices that are easy to describe abstractly but much harder to reproduce intentionally.

04

Reflection

What I notice six years later.

Looking back, my videography was still rough and the audio edit was not as controlled as I would want it to be now. Those issues sometimes compete with the visual idea.

I still appreciate the process I followed: research, observation, planning, making, and reflection. I would happily revisit the concept with stronger sound design and more deliberate coverage.

Technical notes / methodology

The reference study focused on handheld camera motion, colour, and the tonal contrast between characters. This was an exercise in interpreting those choices, not recreating Tarantino’s scene.
